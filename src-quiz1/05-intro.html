MThe maths the course uses

These are the maths tools every page of these notes relies on, each with a small example. Later sections link back here with labels such as "M3" or "M7".

M1 · Averages and the angle brackets ⟨ ⟩

Angle brackets around something mean "the average of it". If five people have 2, 3, 3, 2 and 2 friends, the average number of friends is

\[\langle k\rangle = \frac{2+3+3+2+2}{5} = \frac{12}{5} = 2.4 .\]

\(\langle k^2\rangle\) means "square each number first, then average": \((4+9+9+4+4)/5 = 30/5 = 6\). Note that \(\langle k^2\rangle\) (6) is not the same as \(\langle k\rangle^2\) (\(2.4^2 = 5.76\)). The difference between them measures how spread out the numbers are. It is called the variance:

\[\operatorname{var}(k) = \langle k^2\rangle - \langle k\rangle^2 = 6 - 5.76 = 0.24 .\]

If every number were the same, the variance would be 0. The square root of the variance is the standard deviation \(\sigma\) (Greek "sigma"), a typical distance from the average.

M2 · The summation sign Σ

\(\sum\) (capital Greek "sigma") means "add up". The small text under and over it says what to add.

\[\sum_{k=1}^{4} k = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10, \qquad \sum_{k=1}^{3} k^2 = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14 .\]

Two sums that come up in path-length questions: \(1 + 2 + \dots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}\) and \(1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + n^2 = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\). Check the first with \(n = 4\): \(4\times5/2 = 10\).

An average can be written with a sum. If \(P(k)\) is the fraction of people with \(k\) friends, then \(\langle k\rangle = \sum_k k\,P(k)\). In the example, 3 out of 5 have 2 friends and 2 out of 5 have 3, so \(\langle k\rangle = 2\times\frac35 + 3\times\frac25 = 1.2 + 1.2 = 2.4\), the same as before.

M3 · Probability

  • A probability is a number from 0 (never happens) to 1 (always happens). A fair coin lands heads with probability \(\frac12\).
  • "Not": if something happens with probability \(p\), it fails to happen with probability \(1 - p\).
  • "And" for independent events: multiply. Two heads in a row: \(\frac12\times\frac12 = \frac14\). "Independent" means one result does not affect the other.
  • "Or" for events that cannot both happen: add. A die shows 1 or 2 with probability \(\frac16 + \frac16 = \frac13\).
  • The expected value is the long-run average. If you flip a coin 10 times, the expected number of heads is \(10\times\frac12 = 5\). In general, \(n\) tries each succeeding with probability \(p\) give \(np\) successes on average.
  • \(\binom{n}{k}\), read "n choose k", counts the ways to pick \(k\) things out of \(n\). \(\binom{4}{2} = 6\): from A, B, C, D the pairs are AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD. The formula is \(\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\), where \(n! = n\times(n-1)\times\dots\times1\). The number of pairs among \(N\) nodes is \(\binom N2 = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}\).

M4 · Powers

\(k^3 = k\times k\times k\). A negative power means "one over": \(k^{-2} = \frac{1}{k^2}\), so \(2^{-2} = \frac14\) and \(10^{-2} = 0.01\). Rules: \(k^a\times k^b = k^{a+b}\) and \(\frac{k^a}{k^b} = k^{a-b}\). For large \(k\), \(k^{-2}\) is tiny, \(k^{0} = 1\) always, and \(k^{2}\) is huge. So a large number raised to a negative power goes towards 0, and raised to a positive power grows without limit.

M5 · The number e, exponentials and ln

\(e \approx 2.718\) is a fixed number, like \(\pi\). \(e^{x}\) grows fast as \(x\) increases, and \(e^{-x}\) shrinks towards 0: \(e^{-1} \approx 0.37\), \(e^{-2}\approx 0.14\), \(e^{-5}\approx 0.007\). Also \(e^0 = 1\).

\(\ln\) (the natural logarithm) undoes \(e\): \(\ln(e^x) = x\). It turns multiplying into adding and powers into multiplying: \(\ln(ab) = \ln a + \ln b\), \(\ln(a^n) = n\ln a\). It grows very slowly: \(\ln 10 \approx 2.3\), \(\ln 1000 \approx 6.9\), \(\ln 10^6 \approx 13.8\). The base-10 logarithm \(\log_{10}\) works the same way, with \(\log_{10}100 = 2\).

Three approximations are used in the derivations. You can use them without proof.

  1. For large \(n\), \(\left(1 - \frac{x}{n}\right)^{n} \approx e^{-x}\). Check with \(x = 1\), \(n = 100\): \(0.99^{100} = 0.366\), and \(e^{-1} = 0.368\).
  2. For small \(x\), \(e^{-x} \approx 1 - x + \frac{x^2}{2}\). Check with \(x = 0.1\): \(e^{-0.1} = 0.9048\), and \(1 - 0.1 + 0.005 = 0.905\).
  3. For small \(p\), \(\ln(1-p) \approx -p\). Check: \(\ln 0.99 = -0.01005\).

M6 · Derivatives (slopes) and "dot" notation

The derivative of a function is its slope: how fast it goes up or down. \(f'(x)\) or \(\frac{df}{dx}\) both mean "the slope of \(f\) at \(x\)". A positive slope means the curve is going up, negative means going down.

  • Slope of \(x^n\) is \(n\,x^{n-1}\). So \(x^2\) has slope \(2x\), \(x^3\) has slope \(3x^2\), and \(5x\) has slope 5. A constant has slope 0.
  • Slope of \(e^{cx}\) is \(c\,e^{cx}\).
  • Slopes of a sum add: the slope of \(rx - x^2\) is \(r - 2x\).

A dot over a letter means "rate of change over time". \(\dot x = \frac{dx}{dt}\). If \(x\) is a population, \(\dot x = 3\) means it is growing by 3 per unit of time.

M7 · Integrals (adding up continuous things)

An integral \(\int_a^b f(k)\,dk\) is the area under the curve \(f(k)\) between \(k = a\) and \(k = b\). You can think of it as a sum \(\sum\) over a smooth range of values instead of whole numbers. The course replaces sums over degrees by integrals because integrals are easier to work out. Your syllabus note "not discrete!" refers to this.

The only rule you need is the reverse of the power rule for slopes:

\[\int k^{a}\,dk = \frac{k^{a+1}}{a+1} \qquad (\text{for } a \ne -1).\]

To use limits, work out the answer at the top limit and subtract its value at the bottom limit. This is written with square brackets:

\[\int_1^2 k^2\,dk = \left[\frac{k^3}{3}\right]_1^2 = \frac{8}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac73 .\]

When the top limit is infinity

Power-law questions integrate up to \(\infty\) (infinity). The answer depends on whether \(k^{a+1}\) shrinks or grows as \(k\) gets huge (see M4).

  • \(\int_1^\infty k^{-2}\,dk = \left[\frac{k^{-1}}{-1}\right]_1^\infty = \left[-\frac1k\right]_1^\infty = 0 - (-1) = 1\). At \(k=\infty\), \(1/k\) is 0. The area is finite.
  • \(\int_1^\infty k^{-1/2}\,dk = \left[2k^{1/2}\right]_1^\infty\). At \(k=\infty\), \(\sqrt k\) is infinite. The area is infinite. We say the integral diverges.

So the integral up to infinity is finite only when the power \(a+1\) is negative. This single fact decides every "when does it diverge?" question in §1.

M8 · The one differential equation you must know

A differential equation tells you the rate of change of something in terms of its current value. The simplest one says "the rate of growth is proportional to the current amount":

\[\dot y = a\,y \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y(t) = y(0)\,e^{a t}\]

You can check it: the slope of \(y(0)e^{at}\) is \(a\,y(0)e^{at} = a\,y\). If \(a > 0\), \(y\) grows exponentially. If \(a < 0\), \(y\) shrinks to 0. Almost every stability question in Parts 3 and 4 comes down to "is this \(a\) positive or negative?".

M9 · Matrices, eigenvectors and eigenvalues

A matrix is a table of numbers. A vector is a single column of numbers. Multiplying a matrix by a vector: each entry of the answer is a row of the matrix multiplied entry by entry with the vector, then added.

\[\begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\ 1 & 2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}2\cdot1 + 1\cdot1\\ 1\cdot1 + 2\cdot1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}3\\ 3\end{pmatrix} = 3\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}.\]

Here the matrix did not change the direction of \((1,1)\), it only stretched it by 3. Such a special vector is an eigenvector, and the stretch factor 3 is its eigenvalue. Written generally, \(M\mathbf v = \lambda\mathbf v\) (\(\lambda\) is Greek "lambda"). The same matrix has a second eigenvector \((1,-1)\), with eigenvalue 1, since the result is \((2-1,\ 1-2) = (1,-1)\).

Why this matters: in Part 4, a network's behaviour is split into these special directions. Along each eigenvector, the problem becomes the single equation \(\dot y = a y\) of M8, which we can solve. For a symmetric matrix (one that equals its mirror image across the diagonal), the eigenvectors are at right angles to each other ("orthogonal"), which is what lets the problem split cleanly.
