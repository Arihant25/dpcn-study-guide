How to use this page

Reading the guides again and again won't make them stick. Testing yourself will. So here's the plan.

  1. Read the formula lists below once. Don't try to memorise them yet.
  2. Read the two questions and the recipe. They tie most of the formulas together, so you have far fewer separate things to remember than it looks.
  3. Go to the flashcards. Say the answer out loud (or write it) before you tap "Show answer". Cards you miss come back later in the round.
  4. When a card keeps beating you, follow its link to the full explanation in the guides.

Ten minutes of flashcards a day beats two hours of rereading the night before. (Annoying, but true.)

The two questions behind most answers

That's a lot of formulas. The good news is that most exam questions after class 4 ask one of just two things. Once you see this, many of the formulas above stop being separate facts.

Question 1 · Is it stable?

Nudge the system a little. If it comes back, it's stable. If it runs away, it's unstable. The test is always "look at the slope at the fixed point". Only the kind of slope changes.

Kind of systemStable when
1-D flow x˙=f(x)f′(x∗)<0
2-D or 3-D flowEvery eigenvalue of the Jacobian has negative real part (in 2-D, τ<0 and Δ>0)
Map xn+1=f(xn)|f′(x∗)|<1
Large random systemSCσ2<d (May)

Why is a map different? A flow adds a little bit each instant, so the sign of the slope matters. A map multiplies the error by f′ every step, so its size matters. Multiply by 0.5 over and over and you shrink to nothing. Multiply by 2 and you blow up.

Question 2 · Where is the threshold?

Most "critical values" in the course come from one ratio reaching 1. Below it, nothing happens. Above it, something takes off.

What happensThreshold
A giant component appears⟨k⟩=1
A disease (SIS) spreadsβ/γ=1
A disease spreads on a networkβκ1/γ=1
A map's fixed point stops being stable|f′(x∗)|=1 (logistic map, r=3)
The Lorenz origin stops being stableρ=1
Two oscillators lock together|Δω|/(k1+k2)=1, because sin⁡ϕ can't go past 1
A large system stops being stableSCσ2/d=1

For flows the threshold is a slope reaching 0 instead: the Hopf bifurcation happens at μ=0, and every 1-D bifurcation happens where f′=0. The power-law exponents 2 and 3 are the odd ones out. They're where ⟨k⟩ and ⟨k²⟩ stop being finite.

One recipe for stability

This one recipe answers the SIS question, every bifurcation question, rabbits vs sheep, predator–prey, Lorenz, the Hopf question and the two-oscillator question. Learn it once and you've learnt half the paper.

  1. Find the fixed points. Set every rate to zero and solve.
  2. Take the slope. In 1-D that's f′(x). In 2-D or 3-D it's the Jacobian J.
  3. Put each fixed point in. In 1-D read the sign. In 2-D work out τ and Δ.
  4. Say what it means. "Stable, so the disease settles at 1−γ/β", or "saddle, so the species can't coexist".

For a map, step 3 checks |f′|<1 instead. Even the two-oscillator question is a 1-D problem in disguise, because the phase difference ϕ obeys a single equation.

Six derivations, the short way

Past papers ask for these most often. Each one is written as a chain of short lines, with the reason after "∵". Cover the page, write the chain from memory, then check. The full versions are in the guides.

1 · SIS model

  • I˙=βSI−γIinfections minus recoveries
  • S=1−Ieveryone is either S or I
  • I˙=(β−γ)I−βI2
  • I=0 or I∗=1−γ/βset I˙=0
  • f′(0)=β−γslope at I=0
  • If β<γ, I=0 is stable, so the disease dies out. If β>γ it settles at I∗.

Full version with the solution I(t): Mid-sem guide §11

2 · Power-law constant and average

  • ∫kmin⁡∞Ck−γdk=1probabilities add up to 1
  • Ckmin⁡1−γγ−1=1k1−γ→0 at infinity only if γ>1
  • C=(γ−1)kmin⁡γ−1
  • ⟨k⟩=∫k⋅Ck−γdk=γ−1γ−2kmin⁡same integral with one more k, needs γ>2
  • ⟨k2⟩=γ−1γ−3kmin⁡2one more k again, needs γ>3

Spot the pattern? Each extra power of k knocks 1 off the bottom. Real networks have 2<γ<3, so ⟨k⟩ is finite but ⟨k²⟩ blows up.

Full version: Mid-sem guide §1

3 · Giant component

  • u=1−Su = chance a node is outside the giant component
  • u=(1−p+pu)N−1every other node is either not linked to it, or linked but also outside
  • u=(1−⟨k⟩SN−1)N−1→e−⟨k⟩Sp=⟨k⟩/(N−1) and (1−x/n)n→e−x
  • S=1−e−⟨k⟩S
  • The right side has slope ⟨k⟩ at S=0, so it crosses the line y=S again only if ⟨k⟩>1.

Full version: Mid-sem guide §5

4 · Stability of a map

  • xn=x∗+εna small error
  • x∗+εn+1≈f(x∗)+f′(x∗)εnvalue plus slope times change
  • εn+1=f′(x∗)εnx∗=f(x∗) cancels
  • εn=[f′(x∗)]nε0, which shrinks only if |f′(x∗)|<1
  • Logistic map: f′(1−1/r)=2−r, so it's stable for 1<r<3

Full version: After mid-sem guide §12

5 · Limit cycle and Hopf bifurcation

  • z˙=(μ+iω−|z|2)z, with z=reiθ
  • z˙=(r˙+irθ˙)eiθproduct rule
  • r˙+irθ˙=(μ−r2)r+iωrcancel eiθ
  • r˙=μr−r3, θ˙=ωmatch real and imaginary parts
  • For μ>0: r=0 unstable (slope μ), r=μ stable (slope −2μ). That circle is the limit cycle.

Full version: After mid-sem guide §9

6 · Two coupled oscillators

  • θ˙1=ω1+k1sin⁡(θ2−θ1), θ˙2=ω2+k2sin⁡(θ1−θ2)
  • ϕ˙=(ω2−ω1)−(k1+k2)sin⁡ϕϕ=θ2−θ1, subtract
  • sin⁡ϕ∗=ω2−ω1k1+k2locked means ϕ stops changing
  • |ω2−ω1|≤k1+k2a sine can't go past 1
  • Ω=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2multiply the equations by k2 and k1, add, and sin⁡ϕ∗ cancels

Flashcards

Answer in your head (or on paper) first, then tap "Show answer" and be honest with yourself. A card you didn't know goes back into the pile, so it comes up again before the round ends.

All the cards are also listed below, if you'd rather scroll.

Page 1What does the handshaking lemma say?›

∑iki=2L. Every link has two ends, so it adds 2 to the total degree. That's why ⟨k⟩=2L/N.

Quiz 1 guide §3

Page 1What does [An]ij count?›

The number of walks of length n from i to j. So [A2]ii=ki, and the number of triangles is 16tr⁡(A3).

Quiz 1 guide §4

Page 1Why divide tr⁡(A3) by 6 to count triangles?›

Each triangle shows up as a closed walk from each of its 3 corners, in 2 directions. 3×2=6.

Quiz 1 guide §4

Page 1When does a connected graph have an Euler circuit? An Euler trail?›

Circuit if every node has even degree. Trail (start and end differ) if exactly 2 nodes have odd degree, and it runs between them. More than 2 odd nodes means neither.

Quiz 1 guide §5

Page 1Formula for the clustering coefficient of node i?›

Ci=2Liki(ki−1), the links among its neighbours divided by the most there could be. A complete graph has C=1. A tree has C=0.

Quiz 1 guide §7

Page 1Name the four centralities and what each measures.›

Degree (how many links), closeness (1/∑d, how near everyone is), betweenness (how many shortest paths pass through you), eigenvector (A𝐱=λmax⁡𝐱, linked to important nodes).

Quiz 1 guide §8

Page 1How many links does a tree on N nodes have? A complete graph?›

A tree has N−1. A complete graph has N(N−1)/2.

Quiz 1 guide §9

Page 1Write one step of Euler's method. Which derivative formula is more accurate, forward or central?›

yn+1=yn+hf(yn). Central is more accurate, with error O(h2) against O(h) for forward.

Quiz 1 guide §10

Page 2For P(k)=Ck−γ, what is C?›

C=(γ−1)kmin⁡γ−1, from making the probabilities add up to 1.

Mid-sem guide §1

Page 2For a power law, when are ⟨k⟩ and ⟨k²⟩ finite? Where do real networks sit?›

⟨k⟩ needs γ>2. ⟨k²⟩ needs γ>3. Real networks usually have 2<γ<3, so the average is finite but ⟨k²⟩ blows up.

Mid-sem guide §1

Page 2Why use log binning for a power law?›

With equal-width bins, the tail bins hold 0 or 1 node, so the plot is noisy and zeros can't go on a log axis. Log bins grow by a fixed factor, so the wide tail bins collect enough nodes. Divide each count by N times the bin width.

Mid-sem guide §2

Page 2Average path length of a star, an open chain and a ring?›

Star 2(N−1)/N (about 2). Chain (N+1)/3. Ring about N/4. Ring where each node has degree k: about N/(2k).

Mid-sem guide §3

Page 2In an ER graph, how are p, ⟨k⟩ and N related? What is the degree distribution?›

⟨k⟩=p(N−1). Binomial, which becomes Poisson e−⟨k⟩⟨k⟩k/k! for large N and small p. Its variance equals ⟨k⟩.

Mid-sem guide §4

Page 2Equation for the giant component, and when does it exist?›

S=1−e−⟨k⟩S. It exists only when ⟨k⟩>1. Just above that, S≈2(⟨k⟩−1), so the exponent β=1.

Mid-sem guide §5

Page 2When is an ER graph fully connected?›

When ⟨k⟩>ln⁡N.

Mid-sem guide §5

Page 2ER graph: average path length and clustering? How do real networks compare?›

⟨l⟩≈ln⁡N/ln⁡⟨k⟩ and C=p=⟨k⟩/N. Real networks also have short paths, but much higher clustering.

Mid-sem guide §6

Page 2Is ⟨knn⟩ equal to ⟨k⟩?›

No. ⟨knn⟩=⟨k2⟩/⟨k⟩≥⟨k⟩, because popular nodes show up on many friend lists. For ER it's ⟨k⟩+1.

Mid-sem guide §7

Page 2How do you test whether a fixed point of x˙=f(x) is stable?›

Stable if f′(x∗)<0, unstable if f′(x∗)>0.

Mid-sem guide §8

Page 2Give the normal form of each of the four 1-D bifurcations.›

Saddle-node r−x2 (two points meet and vanish). Transcritical rx−x2 (two points swap stability). Supercritical pitchfork rx−x3 (one point splits into three). Subcritical pitchfork rx+x3.

Mid-sem guide §9

Page 2What is hysteresis?›

With two stable states (stable, unstable, stable), the system jumps at one value of the parameter going up and at a different value coming down. Turning the knob back doesn't undo the jump. The jumps happen at folds, where f=0 and f′=0.

Mid-sem guide §10

Page 2SIS: endemic level, and when does the disease die out?›

I∗=1−γ/β. It dies out when β<γ, because then f′(0)=β−γ<0.

Mid-sem guide §11

Page 2Define the Laplacian. What do its zero eigenvalues tell you?›

L=D−A. The number of zero eigenvalues equals the number of connected components. All eigenvalues are ≥0.

Mid-sem guide §12

Page 2On a network, when does SIS spread?›

When β/γ>1/κ1, where κ1 is the largest eigenvalue of A. Early on the infection grows like e(βκ1−γ)t𝐯1.

Mid-sem guide §13

Page 3For 𝐱˙=A𝐱, what equation gives the eigenvalues?›

λ2−τλ+Δ=0, with τ=a+d (trace) and Δ=ad−bc (determinant).

After mid-sem guide §1

Page 3Classify a 2-D fixed point from τ and Δ.›

Δ<0: saddle. Otherwise the sign of τ gives stability (negative is stable), and τ2>4Δ gives a node, τ2<4Δ a spiral. τ=0 with Δ>0 is a center.

After mid-sem guide §2

Page 3What is the Jacobian, and how does it decide stability?›

Jij=∂fi/∂xj, evaluated at the fixed point. If every eigenvalue has negative real part, it's stable.

After mid-sem guide §5

Page 3Rabbits vs sheep: what kind of point is coexistence, and what does it mean?›

(1,1) is a saddle (λ=−1±2). The stable states are "rabbits only" and "sheep only", so one species always wins. This is competitive exclusion.

After mid-sem guide §6

Page 3Predator–prey (Lotka–Volterra): fixed points and their types?›

(0,0) is a saddle. (d/c, a/b) is a center with λ=±iad, so the populations cycle around it.

After mid-sem guide §7

Page 3Write z˙=(μ+iω−|z|2)z in polar form. What happens at μ=0?›

r˙=μr−r3, θ˙=ω. For μ>0 a stable limit cycle of radius μ appears and the origin turns unstable. That's a supercritical Hopf bifurcation.

After mid-sem guide §9

Page 3What does the Poincaré–Bendixson theorem say?›

In 2-D, if paths are trapped in a region that has no fixed point, there must be a closed orbit in it. It only works in 2-D, which is why chaos needs at least 3 dimensions.

After mid-sem guide §10

Page 3How does Euler's method with Δt=1 turn a logistic equation into the logistic map?›

x˙=x(K−1)−Kx2 becomes xn+1=xn+(K−1)xn−Kxn2=Kxn(1−xn).

After mid-sem guide §11

Page 3When is a fixed point of a map stable? Apply it to the logistic map.›

When |f′(x∗)|<1. For xn+1=rxn(1−xn), the point 1−1/r has f′=2−r, so it's stable for 1<r<3.

After mid-sem guide §12

Page 3Logistic map: where does the period double, and where does chaos start?›

Period 2 at r=3, period 4 at 3.449, period 8 at 3.544. Chaos from about 3.57.

After mid-sem guide §13

Page 3Lorenz system: the fixed points, and when is the origin stable?›

(0,0,0), stable iff ρ<1. For ρ>1 also (±β(ρ−1), ±β(ρ−1), ρ−1). Chaos above about ρ=24.74 (with the usual σ=10, β=8/3).

After mid-sem guide §14

Page 3Two coupled oscillators: when do they lock, and at what frequency?›

They lock when |ω2−ω1|≤k1+k2. The common frequency is Ω=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2, the plain average if k1=k2.

After mid-sem guide §15

Page 3What is the Kuramoto order parameter, and what is Kc?›

reiψ=1N∑jeiθj. r near 0 means out of sync, near 1 means in sync. Sync starts at Kc=2/(πg(0)), which is 2γ for a Lorentzian.

After mid-sem guide §16

Page 3State May's stability criterion.›

A large random system with S species, connectance C, interaction spread σ and self-damping d is stable if SCσ2<d. Bigger or more connected systems are less stable.

After mid-sem guide §17

Page 3Double pendulum with equal masses: the two normal-mode frequencies?›

ω=g/L2±2.

After mid-sem guide §18
