Symbols used everywhere
  • \(\dot x\) (x dot)How fast \(x\) changes with time, \(dx/dt\). Two dots, \(\ddot x\), is how fast \(\dot x\) changes (acceleration).
  • \(x^*\) (x star)A fixed point: a value where nothing changes any more (\(\dot x = 0\), or \(x_{n+1} = x_n\) for a map).
  • \(f'(x)\)The slope (derivative) of \(f\) at \(x\). In \(\dot x = f(x)\), \(f\) is the rule that gives the rate of change.
  • \(\partial f/\partial x\)Partial derivative: the slope of \(f\) as \(x\) changes, holding the other variables fixed.
  • \(\lambda\) (lambda)An eigenvalue. Near a fixed point, a small push grows or shrinks like \(e^{\lambda t}\), so negative means it dies out (stable).
  • \(i\)\(\sqrt{-1}\). Also \(e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta\), a point at angle \(\theta\) on the unit circle.
  • \(\langle\ \rangle\)"The average of". \(\langle k\rangle\) is the average degree, \(\langle k^2\rangle\) the average of the squared degrees.
  • iff"If and only if": the condition works both ways.
