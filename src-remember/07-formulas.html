Formulas to remember

Every formula the course uses, grouped by page. Know these by heart, because you need them to start a proof and to box its answer. Under each formula, "where" says what every letter in it means. Each one is proved further down in Every proof, the short way.

Page 1 · Network basics (classes 1 to 6)

  • Degree of node \(i\)\(k_i = \sum_j A_{ij}\)where \(A\) is the adjacency matrix: \(A_{ij} = 1\) if nodes \(i\) and \(j\) are linked, 0 if not. \(k_i\) (the degree) is how many links node \(i\) has, so it's the sum of row \(i\).
  • Handshaking lemma\(\sum_i k_i = 2L\), so \(\langle k\rangle = 2L/N\)where \(L\) is the number of links, \(N\) the number of nodes, and \(\langle k\rangle\) the average degree.
  • Walks\([A^n]_{ij}\) = number of walks of length \(n\) from \(i\) to \(j\)where \(A^n\) is \(A\) multiplied by itself \(n\) times, and \([A^n]_{ij}\) is its entry in row \(i\), column \(j\). A walk of length \(n\) is \(n\) steps along links (repeats allowed).
  • Triangles\(\frac16\operatorname{tr}(A^3)\)where \(\operatorname{tr}\) (trace) means the sum of the diagonal entries.
  • Euler paths0 odd-degree nodes gives a circuit, 2 gives a trail, more gives nonewhere an Euler trail uses every link exactly once, and a circuit is a trail that ends where it started.
  • Clustering of node \(i\)\(C_i = \dfrac{2L_i}{k_i(k_i-1)}\)where \(L_i\) is the number of links between \(i\)'s neighbours, and \(k_i(k_i-1)/2\) is the number of pairs of neighbours. \(C_i\) runs from 0 (no friends know each other) to 1 (all do).
  • Closeness\(1/\sum_j d_{ij}\)where \(d_{ij}\) is the distance from \(i\) to \(j\): the fewest links you must cross.
  • Betweenness\(\sum_{s,t}\sigma_{st}(v)/\sigma_{st}\)where \(\sigma_{st}\) (sigma) is the number of shortest paths from \(s\) to \(t\), and \(\sigma_{st}(v)\) how many of them pass through node \(v\).
  • Eigenvector centrality\(A\mathbf x = \lambda_{\max}\mathbf x\)where \(\mathbf x\) lists every node's score and \(\lambda_{\max}\) is the largest eigenvalue of \(A\). A node scores high if its neighbours do.
  • Complete graph, tree\(N(N-1)/2\) links, and \(N-1\) linkswhere a complete graph has every pair linked, and a tree is connected with no loops.
  • Euler's method\(y_{n+1} = y_n + h\,f(y_n)\)where the equation is \(\dot y = f(y)\), \(h\) is the step size, and \(y_n\) is the estimate after \(n\) steps.
  • Derivative errorsForward difference \(O(h)\), central difference \(O(h^2)\)where forward is \(\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\) and central is \(\frac{f(x+h) - f(x-h)}{2h}\). \(O(h)\) means the error shrinks in proportion to \(h\), and \(O(h^2)\) in proportion to \(h^2\) (faster).

Page 2 · Mid-sem topics

  • Power law\(P(k) = Ck^{-\gamma}\), with \(C = (\gamma-1)k_{\min}^{\gamma-1}\)where \(P(k)\) is the fraction of nodes with degree \(k\), \(\gamma\) (gamma) is the exponent, \(k_{\min}\) the smallest degree, and \(C\) the constant that makes all the fractions add up to 1.
  • Its averages\(\langle k\rangle = \frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma-2}k_{\min}\) (needs \(\gamma>2\)), \(\langle k^2\rangle = \frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma-3}k_{\min}^2\) (needs \(\gamma>3\))where \(\langle k\rangle\) is the average degree and \(\langle k^2\rangle\) the average of the squared degrees. If the condition fails, that average is infinite.
  • On log-log axes\(\log P = \log C - \gamma\log k\), a straight line of slope \(-\gamma\)where both axes are logarithmic, so the plot is \(\log P\) against \(\log k\).
  • Average path lengthStar \(\frac{2(N-1)}{N}\), chain \(\frac{N+1}{3}\), ring \(\approx\frac N4\), ring with degree \(k\) \(\approx\frac{N}{2k}\)where \(\langle l\rangle\) is the average distance over all pairs of nodes, \(N\) the number of nodes, and \(k\) how many neighbours each ring node has.
  • Random (ER) graph\(\langle k\rangle = p(N-1)\), Poisson \(P(k) = e^{-\langle k\rangle}\frac{\langle k\rangle^k}{k!}\), variance = ⟨k⟩where each pair of the \(N\) nodes is linked with probability \(p\), \(k! = k(k-1)\cdots1\), and the variance \(\langle k^2\rangle - \langle k\rangle^2\) measures how spread out the degrees are.
  • Giant component\(S = 1 - e^{-\langle k\rangle S}\). Exists if \(\langle k\rangle>1\). Near it \(S\approx2(\langle k\rangle-1)\)where \(S\) is the fraction of nodes in the giant component (the one big connected piece).
  • Fully connectedwhen \(\langle k\rangle > \ln N\)where \(\ln\) is the natural log.
  • ER distances, clustering\(\langle l\rangle\approx\frac{\ln N}{\ln\langle k\rangle}\), and \(C = p = \langle k\rangle/N\)where \(\langle l\rangle\) is the average distance and \(C\) the clustering coefficient.
  • Friendship paradox\(\langle k_{nn}\rangle = \langle k^2\rangle/\langle k\rangle\). For ER it's \(\langle k\rangle + 1\)where \(k_{nn}\) is the average degree of a node's neighbours.
  • 1-D stabilityFixed point \(f(x^*) = 0\). Stable if \(f'(x^*)<0\)where the system is \(\dot x = f(x)\).
  • The four bifurcationsSaddle-node \(r - x^2\), transcritical \(rx - x^2\), pitchfork \(rx - x^3\), subcritical \(rx + x^3\)where each is the right side \(f(x)\) of \(\dot x = f(x)\), and \(r\) is the knob you turn. A bifurcation is where the fixed points change as \(r\) passes 0.
  • SIS\(\dot I = (\beta-\gamma)I - \beta I^2\), endemic level \(I^* = 1 - \gamma/\beta\), dies out if \(\beta<\gamma\)where \(I\) is the fraction infected and \(S = 1 - I\) the fraction susceptible (healthy). \(\beta\) (beta) is the infection rate and \(\gamma\) (gamma) the recovery rate. Here \(\gamma\) is not the power-law exponent.
  • Laplacian\(L = D - A\). Number of zero eigenvalues = number of componentswhere \(D\) is the diagonal matrix of degrees (\(k_i\) on the diagonal, 0 elsewhere). A component is a piece of the network that's connected inside but not to the rest.
  • On a networkDiffusion \(\dot{\mathbf x} = -CL\mathbf x\) ends at the average. SIS spreads if \(\beta/\gamma > 1/\kappa_1\)where \(\mathbf x\) lists the amount at each node, \(C\) is the diffusion rate, and \(\kappa_1\) (kappa) is the largest eigenvalue of \(A\).

Page 3 · After mid-sem (classes 13 to 18)

The Quiz 2 topics have a longer "where" for each formula on the Quiz 2 sheet.

  • 2-D linear system\(\lambda^2 - \tau\lambda + \Delta = 0\), with \(\tau = a + d\), \(\Delta = ad - bc\)where the system is \(\dot x = ax + by\), \(\dot y = cx + dy\). \(\tau\) (tau) is the trace and \(\Delta\) (delta) the determinant of its matrix. \(\lambda\) are its eigenvalues.
  • Classify it\(\Delta<0\) saddle. Otherwise \(\tau<0\) stable, and \(\tau^2>4\Delta\) node, \(\tau^2<4\Delta\) spiral, \(\tau = 0\) centerwhere saddle means in one way and out another, node straight in or out, spiral winding, and center closed loops.
  • Jacobian\(J_{ij} = \partial f_i/\partial x_j\) at the fixed point. Stable if every eigenvalue has negative real partwhere the system is \(\dot x_i = f_i(x_1, x_2, \dots)\). Entry \(J_{ij}\) is how much rate \(f_i\) changes when \(x_j\) changes.
  • Rabbits vs sheepCoexistence point \((1,1)\) is a saddle, so one species winswhere \(\dot x = x(3 - x - 2y)\), \(\dot y = y(2 - x - y)\), with \(x\) = rabbits and \(y\) = sheep.
  • Predator–preyCenter at \((d/c,\ a/b)\), \(\lambda = \pm i\sqrt{ad}\), so populations cyclewhere \(\dot N = N(a - bP)\), \(\dot P = P(cN - d)\). \(N\) = prey, \(P\) = predators. \(a\) prey growth, \(b\) eating rate, \(c\) predator gain from eating, \(d\) predator death rate.
  • Network stability\(\dot{\boldsymbol\epsilon} = (\alpha I + \beta L)\boldsymbol\epsilon\), stable iff \(\alpha<0\) and \(\alpha + \beta\lambda_n<0\)where \(\boldsymbol\epsilon\) (epsilon) lists each node's small push from a state where all nodes are equal. \(\alpha = f'(x^*)\) is each node's own slope, \(\beta = g'(x^*)\) the coupling slope, \(I\) the identity matrix, and \(\lambda_n\) the largest Laplacian eigenvalue.
  • Hopf, polar form\(\dot z = (\mu + i\omega - |z|^2)z\) gives \(\dot r = \mu r - r^3\), \(\dot\theta = \omega\). Cycle of radius \(\sqrt\mu\)where \(z = re^{i\theta}\) is a point in the plane, \(r\) its distance from the origin, \(\theta\) its angle, \(\mu\) (mu) the knob, and \(\omega\) (omega) the turning speed.
  • Map stability\(x^* = f(x^*)\), stable if \(|f'(x^*)|<1\)where the map is \(x_{n+1} = f(x_n)\), with \(x_n\) the value at step \(n\).
  • Logistic map\(x_{n+1} = rx_n(1-x_n)\). \(x^* = 1 - 1/r\) stable for \(1where \(x_n\) is the population as a fraction of the maximum and \(r\) the growth rate (0 to 4).
  • LorenzOrigin stable iff \(\rho<1\). Other points \(\left(\pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho-1)},\ \pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho-1)},\ \rho-1\right)\)where \(\dot x = \sigma(y - x)\), \(\dot y = \rho x - y - xz\), \(\dot z = xy - \beta z\), with \(\sigma\), \(\rho\), \(\beta\) positive constants.
  • Two oscillators\(\dot\phi = \Delta\omega - (k_1+k_2)\sin\phi\). Lock iff \(|\Delta\omega|\le k_1+k_2\). \(\Omega = \frac{k_2\omega_1 + k_1\omega_2}{k_1+k_2}\)where \(\phi = \theta_2 - \theta_1\) is the gap between the two phases, \(\Delta\omega = \omega_2 - \omega_1\) the gap between their natural speeds, \(k_1, k_2\) the coupling strengths, and \(\Omega\) the shared speed once locked.
  • Kuramoto\(re^{i\psi} = \frac1N\sum_j e^{i\theta_j}\), critical coupling \(K_c = \frac{2}{\pi g(0)}\)where \(r\) is how in-sync the \(N\) oscillators are (0 to 1), \(\psi\) (psi) their average phase, \(K\) the coupling strength, and \(g(0)\) the height of the spread of natural frequencies at its centre.
  • May's criterionA large random system is stable if \(\sqrt{SC\sigma^2} < d\)where \(S\) is the number of species, \(C\) the connectance (the fraction of pairs that interact), \(\sigma\) (sigma) the typical interaction strength, and \(d\) how strongly each species limits itself.
  • Double pendulumEqual masses: \(\omega = \sqrt{g/L}\,\sqrt{2\pm\sqrt2}\)where \(\omega\) is the frequency of each normal mode (a pattern where both rods swing at one frequency), \(g\) gravity, and \(L\) the length of each rod.
