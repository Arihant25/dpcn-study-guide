Formulas to remember

Know these by heart. They're where each proof starts and what it boxes at the end. Under each formula, "where" says what every letter in it means. Every one is proved further down.

Symbols used everywhere
  • x˙ (x dot)How fast x changes with time, dx/dt. Two dots, x¨, is how fast x˙ changes (acceleration).
  • x∗ (x star)A fixed point: a value where nothing changes any more (x˙=0, or xn+1=xn for a map).
  • f′(x)The slope (derivative) of f at x. In x˙=f(x), f is the rule that gives the rate of change.
  • ∂f/∂xPartial derivative: the slope of f as x changes, holding the other variables fixed.
  • λ (lambda)An eigenvalue. Near a fixed point, a small push grows or shrinks like eλt, so negative means it dies out (stable).
  • i−1. Also eiθ=cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ, a point at angle θ on the unit circle.
  • ⟨ ⟩"The average of". ⟨k⟩ is the average degree, ⟨k2⟩ the average of the squared degrees.
  • iff"If and only if": the condition works both ways.

1 · 2-D stability and limit cycles

  • Eigenvalue equationλ2−τλ+Δ=0, so λ=τ±τ2−4Δ2where the system is x˙=ax+by, y˙=cx+dy, so its matrix is A=(abcd). τ (tau) =a+d is the trace. Δ (delta) =ad−bc is the determinant. λ are the two eigenvalues of A.
  • ClassifyΔ<0 saddle. Otherwise τ<0 stable, and τ2>4Δ node, τ2<4Δ spiral, τ=0 centerwhere τ and Δ are as above (of J for a nonlinear system). Saddle: paths come in one way and leave another. Node: paths go straight in or out. Spiral: they wind in or out. Center: closed loops forever.
  • JacobianJ=(∂f1/∂x1∂f1/∂x2∂f2/∂x1∂f2/∂x2) at the fixed point. Stable if every eigenvalue has negative real partwhere the system is x˙1=f1(x1,x2), x˙2=f2(x1,x2). Entry Jij is how much rate fi changes when xj changes. Work out J in general, then put in each fixed point.
  • Rabbits vs sheepx˙=x(3−x−2y), y˙=y(2−x−y). (1,1) is a saddle, so one species winswhere x = rabbits, y = sheep. 3 and 2 are their growth rates alone. −x and −y are crowding by their own kind. −2y and −x are competition from the other.
  • Predator–preyN˙=N(a−bP), P˙=P(cN−d). Center at (d/c, a/b), λ=±iadwhere N = prey, P = predators. a = prey growth rate, b = how fast predators eat prey, c = how fast predators grow from eating, d = predator death rate. All positive.
  • Hopf normal formz˙=(μ+iω−|z|2)z gives r˙=μr−r3, θ˙=ωwhere z=x+iy=reiθ is a point in the plane. r=|z| is its distance from the origin and θ (theta) its angle. μ (mu) is the control knob. ω (omega) is how fast it turns.
  • Limit cycleRadius μ, stable for μ>0. Origin eigenvalues μ±iω. Supercritical Hopf at μ=0where a limit cycle is a closed loop that nearby paths spiral onto. A Hopf bifurcation is where a stable point turns unstable and a cycle appears around it.
  • Van der Polx¨+μ(x2−1)x˙+x=0where x is the position, x˙ the velocity, x¨ the acceleration, and μ>0 the strength of the "friction" μ(x2−1)x˙. That friction is negative for |x|<1 (pushes swings out) and positive for |x|>1 (damps them), so a cycle forms in between.
  • Poincaré–BendixsonA trapping region with no fixed point holds a closed orbit. 2-D only, so no chaos in 2-Dwhere a trapping region is an area (usually a ring) that paths can enter but never leave, and a closed orbit is a path that loops back on itself.

2 · Maps

  • Fixed pointx∗=f(x∗)where the map is xn+1=f(xn). xn is the value at step n and f is the rule that gives the next value. x∗ is a value the rule leaves unchanged.
  • StabilityStable iff |f′(x∗)|<1, because εn+1=f′(x∗)εnwhere εn (epsilon) =xn−x∗ is the small distance from the fixed point at step n, and f′(x∗) is the slope of f there.
  • Euler with Δt=1x˙=x(K−1)−Kx2 becomes xn+1=Kxn(1−xn)where Δt is the time step, xn is x after n steps, and K is the growth parameter (it becomes r in the logistic map).
  • Logistic mapxn+1=rxn(1−xn), f′(x)=r(1−2x). x∗=0 stable for r<1. x∗=1−1/r has f′=2−r, stable for 1<r<3where xn is the population at generation n, as a fraction of the maximum (between 0 and 1), and r is the growth rate (between 0 and 4).
  • 2-cyclex±=(r+1)±(r−3)(r+1)2r, born at r=3where x+ and x− are the two values the map jumps between, so f(x+)=x− and f(x−)=x+.
  • Route to chaosPeriod doubles at 3, 3.449, 3.544. Chaos from about 3.57where these numbers are values of r. The period is how many steps the map takes to repeat (1, 2, 4, 8, …).
  • Examplesx2: 0 stable, 1 unstable. cos⁡x: x∗≈0.739, stablewhere these are the maps xn+1=xn2 and xn+1=cos⁡xn.

3 · Lorenz

  • Equationsx˙=σ(y−x), y˙=ρx−y−xz, z˙=xy−βzwhere x,y,z are the three changing quantities (from a model of air rising when heated). σ (sigma), ρ (rho) and β (beta) are positive constants. ρ is the one usually varied. The standard values are σ=10, β=8/3.
  • Fixed points(0,0,0), and for ρ>1: (±β(ρ−1), ±β(ρ−1), ρ−1)where the ± signs go together, giving two points P1 (both +) and P2 (both −). They exist only for ρ>1 because the square root needs ρ−1>0.
  • JacobianJ=(−σσ0ρ−z−1−xyx−β)where row 1 is x˙, row 2 is y˙, row 3 is z˙, and the columns are the slopes with respect to x, y, z. Put in each fixed point.
  • OriginStable iff ρ<1
  • ChaosAbove ρ≈24.74 (for σ=10, β=8/3)

4 · Synchronisation

  • Two oscillatorsθ˙1=ω1+k1sin⁡(θ2−θ1), θ˙2=ω2+k2sin⁡(θ1−θ2)where θ1,θ2 (theta) are the phases, meaning where each oscillator is in its cycle, as an angle. ω1,ω2 (omega) are their natural frequencies, how fast each would turn alone. k1,k2 are the coupling strengths, how hard each is pulled towards the other.
  • Phase differenceϕ˙=(ω2−ω1)−(k1+k2)sin⁡ϕwhere ϕ (phi) =θ2−θ1 is how far apart the two are. Locked (synchronised) means ϕ stops changing.
  • LockingIff |ω2−ω1|≤k1+k2. Equal couplings: kc=|ω2−ω1|/2where kc is the critical coupling, the smallest k that makes them lock when k1=k2=k.
  • Common frequencyΩ=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2where Ω (capital omega) is the shared speed once they're locked.
  • Kuramotoreiψ=1N∑jeiθj, θ˙i=ωi+Krsin⁡(ψ−θi)where there are N oscillators and K is the coupling strength. r is the order parameter: 0 means out of sync, 1 means fully in sync. ψ (psi) is the average phase.
  • Critical couplingKc=2πg(0). Lorentzian: Kc=2γ, r=1−Kc/Kwhere g(ω) is the spread of natural frequencies (how many oscillators have each frequency), centred on 0, so g(0) is its height at the centre. γ (gamma) is the width of a Lorentzian spread. Sync starts once K>Kc.

Proofs

The same short proofs as the Memory sheet, just the ones for this quiz. Learn the shaded idea first, then the steps.

Part 1 · 2-D stability and limit cycles

Solve 𝐱˙=A𝐱 in 2-D, and get the eigenvalue equation λ2−τλ+Δ=0.

Guess eλt𝐯. It works exactly when 𝐯 is an eigenvector.

  • Try 𝐱=eλt𝐯: λeλt𝐯=eλtA𝐯
  • A𝐯=λ𝐯cancel eλt≠0
  • det⁡(A−λI)=(a−λ)(d−λ)−bc=0𝐯≠0
  • λ2−τλ+Δ=0, τ=a+d, Δ=ad−bc, so λ=τ±τ2−4Δ2
  • 𝐱(t)=c1eλ1t𝐯1+c2eλ2t𝐯2linear, so solutions add. c1,c2 from 𝐱(0).

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §1

Classify a 2-D fixed point using τ and Δ.

Read it off λ=τ±τ2−4Δ2. Δ=λ1λ2 and τ=λ1+λ2.

  • Δ<0: eigenvalues real with opposite signs, so a saddleλ1λ2=Δ
  • Δ>0, τ2>4Δ: real, same sign, so a node
  • Δ>0, τ2<4Δ: complex, so a spiral
  • Stable if τ<0, unstable if τ>0τ is the sum (or twice the real part)
  • τ=0, Δ>0: λ=±iΔ, a center

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §2

Show that near a fixed point, a nonlinear system behaves like 𝜹˙=J𝜹.

Same as 1-D: push it a little and Taylor-expand, now with partial derivatives.

  • xi=xi∗+δxismall push
  • δx˙1=f1(𝐱∗)+∂f1∂x1δx1+∂f1∂x2δx2+…Taylor
  • f1(𝐱∗)=0, drop products of small terms
  • 𝜹˙=J𝜹, Jij=∂fi/∂xj at 𝐱∗
  • Stable if every eigenvalue of J has negative real part. Classify with τ, Δ of J.

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §5

Rabbits vs sheep, x˙=x(3−x−2y), y˙=y(2−x−y): find and classify the fixed points.

Four fixed points. The coexistence one is a saddle, so one species always wins.

  • (0,0), (0,2), (3,0), (1,1)one zero factor from each equation
  • J=(3−2x−2y−2x−y2−x−2y)
  • (0,0): λ=3,2, unstable node
  • (3,0): λ=−3,−1, and (0,2): λ=−1,−2. Both stable nodes.triangular matrices, read the diagonal
  • (1,1): Δ=1−2=−1<0, saddle, λ=−1±2
  • Only "rabbits only" or "sheep only" survive: competitive exclusion

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §6

Predator–prey, N˙=N(a−bP), P˙=P(cN−d): show the populations cycle.

The inside fixed point has τ=0 and Δ>0, which is a center.

  • (0,0) and (d/c, a/b)
  • J=(a−bP−bNcPcN−d)
  • (0,0): λ=a,−d, a saddleprey alone grow, predators alone die
  • (d/c,a/b): J=(0−bd/cac/b0), τ=0, Δ=ad
  • λ=±iad, a center, so the populations go round in cycles

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §7

Write z˙=(μ+iω−|z|2)z in polar form, and find when there is a stable limit cycle.

Put z=reiθ, match real and imaginary parts, and you get a 1-D equation for r.

  • z˙=(r˙+irθ˙)eiθproduct rule
  • r˙+irθ˙=(μ−r2)r+iωrcancel eiθ
  • r˙=μr−r3, θ˙=ωmatch real and imaginary parts
  • r∗=0, and r∗=μ if μ>0
  • f′(0)=μ, f′(μ)=−2μf′(r)=μ−3r2
  • μ>0: origin unstable, circle r=μ stable. Supercritical Hopf at μ=0.

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §9

Using the Jacobian, show that the origin of z˙=(μ+iω−|z|2)z has eigenvalues μ±iω.

Write it in x and y, take the Jacobian at the origin, and read off τ and Δ.

  • x˙=μx−ωy−x(x2+y2), y˙=ωx+μy−y(x2+y2)z=x+iy, split real and imaginary parts
  • J(0,0)=(μ−ωωμ)the cubic terms have zero slope at the origin
  • τ=2μ, Δ=μ2+ω2, so λ=μ±iω
  • μ<0: stable spiral. μ>0: unstable spiral. The pair crosses the imaginary axis at μ=0, which is the Hopf bifurcation.

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §9

Use the Poincaré–Bendixson theorem to show that r˙=r−r3, θ˙=ω has a closed orbit.

Find a ring where the flow points inwards on both edges and that holds no fixed point. A path trapped there must approach a closed orbit.

  • Take the ring 0.5≤r≤2
  • Inner edge: r˙=0.5−0.125=0.375>0, the flow points into the ring
  • Outer edge: r˙=2−8=−6<0, the flow points into the ring
  • The only fixed point is the origin, which is outside the ringθ˙=ω≠0, so nothing stops inside
  • So a closed orbit lies in the ring (here it's r=1)Poincaré–Bendixson, valid in 2-D only

The theorem is also why a 2-D system can't be chaotic. Chaos needs at least three dimensions, like Lorenz, or a map.

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §10

Part 2 · Maps

Show that Euler's method with Δt=1 turns x˙=x(K−1)−Kx2 into the logistic map.

Just do one Euler step and simplify.

  • xn+1=xn+Δt[(K−1)xn−Kxn2]Euler step
  • Δt=1: xn+1=Kxn−Kxn2=Kxn(1−xn)
  • Same nonzero fixed point 1−1/K as the equation

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §11

Show that a fixed point of xn+1=f(xn) is stable iff |f′(x∗)|<1. Apply it to the logistic map.

Each step multiplies the error by f′(x∗). Repeated multiplication shrinks only if the number is smaller than 1 in size.

  • xn=x∗+εnsmall error
  • εn+1=f′(x∗)εnTaylor, and x∗=f(x∗) cancels
  • εn=[f′(x∗)]nε0→0 iff |f′(x∗)|<1
  • Logistic: x∗=0 (f′=r) and x∗=1−1/r (f′=2−r)f′(x)=r(1−2x)
  • So 0 is stable for r<1, and 1−1/r for 1<r<3

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §12

Find the fixed points and their stability for the maps f(x)=x2 and f(x)=cos⁡x.

Solve x=f(x), then check |f′|<1 at each point. This was 2024 Quiz 2, Set 1, Q3.

  • x2: x=x2 gives x∗=0 or 1, and f′(x)=2x
  • |f′(0)|=0<1, stable. |f′(1)|=2>1, unstable.from 0.9: 0.81, 0.66, 0.43 → 0. From 1.1: 1.21, 1.46 → ∞
  • cos⁡x: x∗=cos⁡x∗. Iterate from 1: 0.540, 0.858, 0.654, 0.794, … → x∗≈0.739
  • f′(x)=−sin⁡x, |f′(0.739)|=sin⁡0.739≈0.674<1, stablenegative slope, so the iterates zigzag in

Worked answer: After mid-sem guide §19

Show that the logistic map gets a 2-cycle at r=3.

A 2-cycle is a fixed point of the map applied twice. Remove the ordinary fixed points and a quadratic is left.

  • f(x1)=x2, f(x2)=x1, so f(f(x))=x
  • Degree 4. Divide out the roots 0 and 1−1/r.ordinary fixed points also solve it
  • x±=(r+1)±(r−3)(r+1)2r
  • Real only if r≥3the square root needs (r−3)(r+1)≥0
  • Born at r=3, exactly where f′=2−r hits −1: period doubling

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §13

Part 3 · Lorenz

Lorenz system: find the fixed points and show that the origin is stable iff ρ<1.

Solve the three equations from the easy ones first. At the origin the z part separates, leaving a 2×2 block.

  • y=xσ(y−x)=0
  • z=x2/βxy−βz=0
  • x(ρ−1−z)=0, so P0=(0,0,0) or z=ρ−1, x=y=±β(ρ−1)real only for ρ>1
  • J=(−σσ0ρ−z−1−xyx−β)
  • At P0: λ=−β, and λ2+(σ+1)λ+σ(1−ρ)=0
  • Both roots negative iff σ(1−ρ)>0, i.e. ρ<1trace already negative, need positive determinant
  • At P1,2, with c=β(ρ−1): J=(−σσ01−1∓c±c±c−β)put x=y=±c, z=ρ−1, so ρ−z=1

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §14

Part 4 · Synchronisation

Two coupled oscillators: find when they lock and the common frequency Ω.

Subtract to get one equation for the phase difference. Locking means that difference stops changing, and a sine can't exceed 1.

  • θ˙1=ω1+k1sin⁡(θ2−θ1), θ˙2=ω2+k2sin⁡(θ1−θ2)
  • ϕ˙=(ω2−ω1)−(k1+k2)sin⁡ϕϕ=θ2−θ1, subtract
  • sin⁡ϕ∗=ω2−ω1k1+k2locked means ϕ˙=0
  • Lock iff |ω2−ω1|≤k1+k2|sin⁡|≤1
  • Stable root has cos⁡ϕ∗>0slope −(k1+k2)cos⁡ϕ must be negative
  • Ω=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2Ω=ω1+k1sin⁡ϕ∗=ω2−k2sin⁡ϕ∗. Weight and add.

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §15

Rewrite the Kuramoto model with the order parameter, and say which oscillators lock.

Each oscillator only feels the average. The more in sync the crowd, the harder the pull.

  • reiψ=1N∑jeiθjdefinition
  • rsin⁡(ψ−θi)=1N∑jsin⁡(θj−θi)multiply by e−iθi, take imaginary parts
  • θ˙i=ωi+Krsin⁡(ψ−θi)substitute into the coupling sum
  • Set ψ=0: oscillator i stops if sin⁡θi=ωi/(Kr)
  • Locks iff |ωi|≤Kr. The rest keep drifting. Sync starts at Kc=2/(πg(0)).

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §16
